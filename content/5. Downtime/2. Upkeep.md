Ship parts wear out, there are fuel and repair costs, and minor patch jobs to handle scrapes and dents. Each downtime right after payoff, decide if you pay for ship upkeep. Upkeep for a ship is calculated as follows (rounded down):

- **Upkeep = ([[3. The Crew/3. Airships/Ship Systems#System Rating\|All System Ratings]] + [[3. The Crew/5. Crew Quality\|Crew Quality]]) / 4**

## Skipping Upkeep

Sometimes you don’t have the funds (or just don’t want to spend the funds) to upkeep your ship. In that case roll dice equal to the number of upkeeps you’ve skipped (if this is your first, you’re at 1d).

The roll doesn’t guarantee your ship falls apart. If you roll a **1-3** result, the wear and tear is minor and can be paid for during the next upkeep. If you roll a **4/5** result, a system blows but an engineer can put together a stopgap solution with just a **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** roll (maybe you have a replacement part on hand). If you roll a **6** or a **crit**, there will be a significant hit to your ship (as if your ship took a point of [[3. The Crew/3. Airships/Ship Systems#System Damage\|damage]]) as a piece goes critical and affects a whole system.

The outcome of the upkeep roll is known, but doesn’t occur immediately. So if you roll a **6+** you may not realize that a critical part has worn through (otherwise you probably would have repaired and replaced it as part of your upkeep). The problems that occur during these rolls tend to appear when you need a part the most. If you’re trying to run a blockade with some illegal goods next job, your engine might fail. Perhaps a part might fail when you’re trying to land back home after a long job. Whatever the trouble, it’s not immediately apparent, but the GM should make sure that it crops up during the next job that utilizes the ship.

If you need some **[[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Coin]]** fast, you may consider going into [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Coin#Debt\|debt]].
