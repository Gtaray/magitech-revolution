In addition to your own character sheets, you'll also have the crew sheet. The Unlike player characters, where your playbook is the single decision on which your character hinges, the crew gets two such choices. First you'll choose the type of crew you want to be, then you'll choose your starting airship.

There are a lot of questions to answer when you set up your ship, but the most important questions have to do with what kind of story you want to tell. The choices you make during this phase will go the furthest towards determining what themes and conflicts you’ll see in your game.

In this chapter, you’ll learn about the various ships in the game and how to create a crew that you’re excited to tell stories about.

# Choose a Crew Type

Your crew type determines the jobs that you'll focus on, as well as a selection of special abilities and upgrades that support that kind of action. The type isn't meant to be restrictive - the Runners might sometimes engage bounty hunting (like the Professionals) - but the core activity of the type is the most frequent way your crew earns Gold and xp and advancement.

There are three different crew types to choose from

- **[[3. The Crew/2. Crews Archetypes/Professionals\|The Professionals]].** Mercenaries and bounty hunters. When someone needs something (anything) done right, they call on you.
- **[[3. The Crew/2. Crews Archetypes/Renegades\|The Renegades]].** Smugglers, thieves, and brigands. Dealers in the various vices that get people through the day. Criminals, the lot of you.
- **[[3. The Crew/2. Crews Archetypes/Runners\|The Runners]].** Rebels and terrorists. You've got problem with the Industrialist government, maybe several.

Your crew type is how your crew is known out on the Khettish steppes. People will think of you as "bounty hunters", "brigands", "terrorists" and will treat you accordingly.

Choosing your crew type is a very important decision! It's a way for the group to say, "These are the sorts of jobs we want to focus on doing." It organizes gameplay from "doing crimes" to "smuggling illegal magitech" or "protecting the druid circles - which helps the GM focus on the parts of the setting that matter most to your game, rather than having to juggle every possibility at once.

The group should choose a crew type that everyone is excited about. As a player, be vocal about your preferences. You're about to invest many hours into this game, so if you're feeling lukewarm about one of the options, speak up.

# Choose a Ship

Once you've locked in your crew type, it's time to pick your starting ship. Your starting ship won't influence the kinds of jobs you're likely to do, but it will affect how you approach those jobs and the methods most available to you.

There are three different crew types to choose from

- **[[3. The Crew/3. Airships/Augur Class]].** An old mining and prospecting ship. It's a hardy, chunky beast that can resist the elements and get you to out of the way places.
- **[[3. The Crew/3. Airships/Nomad Class]].** Hardly more than an open frame built around a cargo hold and engines. She's lightweight and nimble and ready for any upgrades you want to add.
- **[[3. The Crew/3. Airships/Oracle Class]].** A decommissioned military support ship designed for reconnaissance. She's got infrastructure ready to receive military modules.

# Choose Your Reputation

Your crew has just formed and acquired a airship. Given this group of characters and their previous escapades, what initial reputation would you have among the factions and clans of Duradin? Choose one from the list on the right (or create your own).

- Ambitious
- Brutal
- Daring
- Honorable
- Professional
- Savvy
- Strange
- Subtle

You earn xp when you bolster your airship’s reputation, so think of this as another cue to indicate what sorts of action you want in the game. Will you be recklessly ambitious, targeting higher Tiers? Will you take on daring jobs that others deem too risky? Are you interested in the strange and mystical? Or are you professional in all dealings?

# Customize Your Airship

Each airship starts with several preselected ship systems that reflect its focus. For example, the Nomad class airship starts with a bigger hull to help carry cargo (legal and illegal).

Your ship also begins with **2 Coin** in its hold (those represent the remains of the crew’s savings from adventures and the acquisition of the ship beforehand).

You know get to choose one of the following options:

- Choose two additional ship systems to improve. Your choices are between **engines**, **hull**, **support**, and **weapons**. This will improve the quality of that system. The number of modules you can install in a system is limited by its quality
- Spend your starting **2 Coin** to improve your **crew quality** by 1. You will also owe the folks who helped you improve it. A higher crew quality refers to the quality of gear and equipment of the crew. You’re able to take on more powerful factions on equal footing, and can therefore handle harder jobs more easily. Not to mention, your crew will just have nicer stuff!

After you decide what you want to improve, the GM will tell you about a faction that helped you get those improvements. They did you a favor. How did your crew respond?

- **Pay them off.** Give them **1 Coin** in exchange for a job well done. No strings attached, nothing owed.
- **Owe them one.** Promise them you’ll return the favor down the line when they ask and gain **+1 status** with them. If you chose **crew quality**, you must take this option.
- **Stiff them.** No need to pay a faction that doesn’t demand payment up front! Take **-1 status** with that faction.

# Choose a Special Ability

Choose one of the **special abilities** listed on your crew type. If you can't decide which one to pick, go with the recommended ability — It's recommended for a reason! It’s important to pick a special ability that everyone is excited about. You can get more special abilities in the future by earning crew xp.

Just like picking your ship type, origin, and systems, choosing a special ability is another chance to focus the game. Instead of playing a generic crew crewed by scoundrels, you end up with the Nomad-class and her crew of ambitious smugglers, who salvaged the ship after her previous crew went missing. They stiffed the Dyrinek Gang when they upgraded the hull and weapons of the ship and are known for their Getaway ability—showcasing a knack for running from their problems. That’s a lot to work with, and it helps get the game going in a strong direction from the very beginning!

# Assign Upgrades

In addition to the **upgrades** each ship comes with, you as a group get to add two additional upgrades. For example, you might pick the Professionals’ Crowd Control Gear upgrade, and also the Auxiliary Armory module. That's a crew that loves their weapons!

An upgrade is a valuable asset or system module that helps the crew in some way, such as an Afterburner module or a Dop Hatch. Each ship starts with preselected upgrades that are well suited for that crew, such as the Galley for the Augur-Class or the Intruder Alarm for the Oracle-Class.

After you assign your two upgrades, the GM will tell you about two factions impacted by your choices:

- One faction helped you get an upgrade. You’re on good terms. Did they broker a deal? Did you run a job for them? Did you bail them out of trouble? They like you, and you get **+1 status** with them. At your option, spend **1 Coin** to repay their kindness, and take **+2 status** with them instead.
- The other faction was screwed over when you got an upgrade. Did you steal the part from them? Was it a specific individual’s ship? Was it illegal, and the authorities now hunt them? They don’t like you, and you get **-2 status** with them. At your option, the crew can spend **1 Coin** to mollify them, and take **-1 status** with them instead — tell us how you smoothed things over.

You’ll be able get more upgrades in the future by earning xp or spending **Coin**.

# Choose a Favorite Contact

Take a look at your list of potential contacts on the crew type sheet. Although all the contacts are your friends and allies, one is closer to the crew than the others.

Choose one contact who is a close friend, long-time ally, or partner in crime. The GM will tell you about two factions that are impacted by your choice:

- One faction is also friendly with this contact, and you get **+1 status** with them. Perhaps they are an informant, retired member of the faction, or just have friends among them.
- One faction is unfriendly with this contact, and you get **-1 status** with them. There might be old slights, current grievances, or even materials held against the contact that they are keen to reclaim.

# Update Your Ship Info

An airship has to pay **upkeep** fees every **downtime** or risk damage as parts wear out. To calculate your upkeep costs, add your crew quality to all system quality ratings and divide by four, rounding down. Most ships will have an upkeep of 2 to start.

Your ship creation is done — you’re ready to fly!
