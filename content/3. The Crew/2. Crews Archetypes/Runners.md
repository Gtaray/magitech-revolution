For all its perks, civilization breeds discontent, and where there is misery, there is profit to be made. You're smugglers, thieves, dealers, and brigands, whose job is to provide the unhappy with their vice of choice... That is, if they can pay your hefty price.

To everyone else, you're the problem with society. But we all know you perform a vital service. Without you, the masses get violent.

You could fly in the face of the law, or you could make backroom deals to keep the law away.

**When you play this crew, you earn xp when you complete a job that involved generalized skullduggery (burglary, kidnapping, smuggling, etc).**

*What kind of contraband do you usually deal in, and what do you refuse to sell?*

# Special Abilities
### Cheese It!
You gain **potency** when you **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Scramble]]** or **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Helm]]** to avoid capture or run a blockade. Once you have grabbed the goods, the crew recovers **1 [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress\|stress]]**.

*Sometimes escape is the better part of valor. This ability works for anyone on the crew whether they’re on the ship or not. 'Grabbed the goods' means when the crew has acquired whatever valuable thing you're being hired to acquire.*

### Cargo Eye
Your crew earns **+1 [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Coin]]** for smuggling or delivery jobs. Whenever you **[[1. How to Play/8. Gathering Information\|gather information]]** you can always ask, “What is most valuable here?”

*The extra Coin is gained during payoff beyond what the job pays. You can ask the question whenever it’s applicable.*

### Field Repairs
You gain **potency** when repairing your ship while out in the wilds. If you spend a **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/5. Gambits\|gambit]]** on a **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** roll, you gain **+2d** (instead of **+1d**).

*The potency may functionally let you do repairs others can’t — for example, temporarily fixing a ship injury that usually requires drydock. The extra dice from the gambit apply to any Rig roll, not just repairs.*

### Leverage
Your crew knows how to pull strings and cash in favors. After assigning **[[4. The Job/6. Heat]]** after a job, you can take **-1 [[6. Factions/3. Faction Status\|status]]** with a faction at Helpful (+1) or better to reduce your **[[4. The Job/6. Heat#Wanted Level\|Wanted Level]]** by 1, and set your **[[4. The Job/6. Heat]]** to 0 in a region.

*You can cash in faction changes you gained from the current job. You don’t have to clear the system you did the job in—the faction will help cover your tracks regardless.*

### Just Passing Through
During **payoff**, take **-1 [[4. The Job/6. Heat]]** from the job. When your **[[4. The Job/6. Heat]] is 4** or less in your current region, you still have two **[[5. Downtime/4. Downtime Activities\|downtime activities]]** even if you’re at [[6. Factions/3. Faction Status#Faction Status Levels\|War (-3)]] with any faction, as they have trouble locating you.

*Use the Heat of the region you’re in at the start of downtime to determine the number of activities each crew member gets. If, during downtime, the crew passes through a region in which they have high Heat, enemy factions might find them anyway.*

### Chaos Crew
Your crew gains a new **[[3. The Crew/6. Crew Advancement\|xp trigger]]**: You caused mischief, mayhem, chaos, or confusion unrelated to a job's goals.

*Take a moment to think about how each of your characters cannot help but to cause a little mayhem during a job. Your actions will almost certainly cause problems for you, so why do you do them?*

### Problem Solvers
Each PC may add 1 action rating to **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Helm]]**, **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]**, or **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Scramble]]** (up to a max of 3).

*Each player may choose the action they prefer (you don’t all have to choose the same one). If you take this ability during initial character and crew creation, it supersedes the normal starting limit for action ratings.*

# Crew Upgrades
### False Ship Papers
Every criminal needs a few well-forged documents, giving the crew and ship identities that are less wanted in any given territory. These papers often simplify transit between occupied regions. You have a couple different sets you can swap between, even if you do have to practice responding to a new name at every checkpoint. May make following and tracking you more difficult by enemy factions.

### Lead-Lined Lockbox
A small portion of your ship's storage is lead-lined and warded against divination magic, preventing anyone from detecting what's inside magically.

### Lucky Charm
Whether a family heirloom or a few mementos prominently displayed at the helm, sometimes luck is just about believing in something. The crew starts with **+1 [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/5. Gambits\|gambit]]** every job. It costs two advancements to unlock this instead of the usual one.

### Smuggler's Rigging
Webbing or special harnesses used to hold small items close to the body, and a great way to sneak them past guards. Adds some hands-free carry room while working on the outside of the ship, and lets you smuggle a weapon into a well-guarded meeting while keeping the stylish cut of your coat. You can only carry/hide one item of a max of **1 [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Loadout\|load]]** this way.

### Thrillseekers
Each PC gets **+1 [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress\|stress]]** box (increase stress max to 10). It costs three advancements to unlock, not just one.
