It doesn't matter where you are, who you're working for, or what your personal opinions are, you get it done. The contract, the job, is all that matters. That's what it means to be a professional.

You could be a band of mercs sent in when someone needs to disappear or an insurrection needs to get put down. You could be a shadowy cabal that skulks in the shadows and waits for the perfect moment to strike.

Whatever your means, yours is the promise of violence, and you are paid well for your expertise in this area.

**When you play this crew, you earn xp when you fulfill the requirements of a contract.**

_Who pays your bounties? What mission do you feel guilty about completing?_

# Special Abilities

### Licensed

Take **-2 [[6. Heat|Heat]]** on any legitimate military job. Your ship can be outfitted with most weapons that are normally illegal, and your crew can carry and legally use military magitech while employed by the government.

_This also means that you carry mercenary licenses. Sometimes this can be leveraged to explain being places you generally shouldn’t be, and carry weapons when others may be stripped of them. Law cooperation varies from region to region._

### Forewarned, Forearmed

During a job, the first **[[3. Flashbacks|flashback]]** anyone in the crew initiates costs **0 [[Stress|stress]]**, or if it involves a **[[Work#Long-Term Project|long-term project]]** or **[[Acquire|acquiring an asset]]**, it costs **0 [[Coin]]**.

_Knowing what mess you're about to step in goes a long way in this line of work. Depending on your contacts and leverage, more than a simple gather info check might be required._

### Kick in the Door

When you use an **[[2. Planning & Engagement#Assault|assault]]** plan, you only start in a **[[3. Position#Desperate|desperate]]** position if your **[[2. Planning & Engagement#Engagement Roll|engagement roll]]** is a 1. You can choose to start in a **[[3. Position#Desperate|desperate]]** position after you roll, and if you do, mark a **[[6. Crew Advancement|crew xp]]**.

_You're used to head-on problem solving and are rarely surprised when a fight breaks out. You can use the second ability for any engagement plan, and doing so means more things than usual have gone wrong, and your opposition has some major advantages over you._

### We Were Never Here

When you use a **[[2. Planning & Engagement#Deception|deception]]**, **[[2. Planning & Engagement#Infiltration|infiltration]]**, or **[[2. Planning & Engagement#Social|social]]** plan, add **+1d** to the **[[2. Planning & Engagement#Engagement Roll|engagement roll]]** roll. Earn **+1 [[Coin]]** for jobs done quiet.

_Your crew’s focus is on getting to, and possibly out with, a priority target, and doing so without raising alarms. Any mission where you have to engage a target under watch by their guards applies to this ability._

### Loaded for Bear

Your crew can carry **+1 [[Loadout|load]]**. They have distinctive and high-quality armor. When you wear [[Armor|armor]], it counts as **heavy armor** (two uses).

_Each crew member’s load maximums are increased (so discreet load is 5, and conspicuous load is 7). Who crafted such distinctive armor (and who might recognize its make)? Heavy armor allows you to also tick the second (heavy) armor box on your sheet to reduce appropriate damage_

### Play Both Sides

When you intentionally let a mark slip, make them a crew contact.

_While you may not receive payout from the factions hiring you, many marks have a few coin saved up they’re willing to trade for freedom. More lucrative though are the jobs or hot tips they might have. Add the target as a crew contact, though bear in mind they may someday get captured by other crew (and may pay for a rescue by your crew). Not every mercenary captures their targets, so the GM will (or won’t) adjust faction status as fictionally required at the end of such a job._

### Deadly

Each PC may add 1 action rating to **[[Study]]**, **[[Scrap]]**, or **[[Skulk]]** (up to a max of 3).

_Each player may choose the action they prefer (you don’t all have to choose the same one). If you take this ability during initial character and crew creation, it supersedes the normal starting limit for action ratings._

# Crew Upgrades

### Battle Hardened

Sometimes legality is only a question of who's quickest to the draw. Each crew member gains **+1 [[Stress]] box** (total 10). It costs three advancements to unlock this instead of the usual one.

### Crowd Control Gear

A suite of gear that is designed to help you manage folks and get them to do what you want them to do. Gain **potency** for such actions.

### Hard Knocks

Luck is just hard-earned experience. Your crew starts each job with **+1 [[5. Gambits|gambit]]**. It costs two advancements to unlock this instead of the usual one.

### Tracers

A wide array of ways to track your targets. Includes a variety of divination and detection spells, uniquely attuned scrying targets that can be hidden on clothes with a suave pat on the back, marks that can attach to hulls, and even ways to intercept unprotected messages from a Sending array. Legality varies, often by the importance of the target, but a [[Professionals#Licensed|license]] makes it all legal.

### Triage Training

It's often a necessity to patch someone up out in the field, whether your own crew or a mark after things got a bit too hot. Gain **potency** when **[[Doctor|Doctoring]]** level 1 or 2 [[Harm|harm]] on a [[1. The Job|job]].

# Contacts

- **Arlox**, [[Ashen Knives]] Usan. Once an assassin, now Syndicate leader. Do you also hunt illegal bounties for them?
- **Ishi**, weapons dealer. Bounties come armed, and Ishi evens the field. Do they tune your ship?
- **Jezri**, a fixer. Whatever you need, Jezri has. Info, gear, jobs, friends, and especially enemies. Did Jezri help you get your ship or license?
- **Lix**, bounty hunter. Do you team up for jobs, or compete for the score?
- **Usok**, Ace reporter. Has all sorts of hot leads, but what does she ask for in return?
