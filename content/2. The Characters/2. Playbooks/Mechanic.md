Whether it's fixing up the airship's engines or constructing a specialized hexcracker to break into a magically protected vault, a mechanic is an invaluable asset on most jobs. You might be the mousy one who has all the fancy toys, or more hands-on, lugging your gear to the job. Or you might prefer to literally make friends and specialize in construct creation. When something breaks, you're the one to call.

**When you play a Mechanic, you earn xp when you address challenges with technical skill or ingenuity.** Always look at the devices around you and be prepared to make them do what you want them to.

*Did you make your own scry-drone? How’d you learn your technical skills? Where’d you find your pet and what is it? Are you unassuming, fading into a crowd, or hard to miss, covered in tattoos?*
# Playing a Mechanic
Playing a Mechanic is foremost about your relationship with the [[8. Glossary/Airship\|airship]]. No one else will have the same ability to keep it flying, and when something breaks, all eyes will be on you. Look for opportunities to bring up what you’ve personally modified on the ship. Where do you get parts when the ship needs something repaired?

How did you become a mechanic? Were you mentored by one of your friends? Were you once a [[6. Factions/4. Industrialists/Guild of Engineers\|Guild]] trainee? Why did you leave and join the crew? Do you approach fixing the ship as a stop-gap solution, where you’re simply trying to find a patch until the next thing breaks, or is it a matter of pride that something never fails twice?

Familiarize yourself with the [[5. Downtime/5. Downtime Activities/Craft\|crafting system]]. The ability for you to make new magitech is very powerful, and the starting Tinker ability gives you an advantage that no one else will be able to match. Ask the rest of the crew what devices you might be able to create and get them to chip in for their development, either in extra downtimes to speed up design or with extra [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Coin]] to pay for assembly.

Action-wise, you may want to pick up **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Skulk]]** if you tend to lurk in the background, or **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Attune]]** if you expect to be working with natural magics. If you also serve as the crew’s foremost magitech expert, you’ll want to stack either some **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** or **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Hack]]**.

Veteran ability-wise, the [[2. The Characters/2. Playbooks/Speaker\|Speaker's]] [[2. The Characters/2. Playbooks/Speaker#Old Friends\|Old Friends]] ability can play up your connections among crafters and hackers. If you want to go full mad scientist, look at the [[2. The Characters/2. Playbooks/Stitch\|Stich's]] [[2. The Characters/2. Playbooks/Stitch#Magic Hands\|Magic Hands]] ability.

# Colorful Friends
- **Morkum**, a junkyard owner. What parts do they save for you? Or did you boost something of theirs?
- **Thorgron**, a previous employer. A captain or a business owner? Did you leave on good terms?
- **Nisryn**, a gambler of ill repute. Are you long-standing friends? Did you cheat at their table?
- **Umdek**, a black market dealer. Do they get you the parts no one else can? Did you fail to deliver on something you promised?
- **Ardeth**, a family member. Are they also a mechanic? Were you both vying for a parent's attentions?
# Special Abilities
## Starting Ability
### Tinker
When you work on a clock with **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** or **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Hack]]**, or when you **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Study]]** a schematic, fill **[[1. How to Play/1. The Basics/3. Progress Clocks\|+1 tick]]**.

*You get this bonus segment regardless of whether this is a downtime action or not. This means that bypassing security on a job or doing an emergency patch while escaping a chasing ship is easier for you than others.*

## Special Abilities
### White Mage
When you **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/2. Push Yourself\|resist]]** a magical effect, you can choose to either suffer one less stress, or allow a cremate to **resist** the magical effect as well. You have **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect#Potency\|potency]]** when you **[[1. How to Play/8. Gathering Information\|gather info]]** on a magical effect or spell.

*You're always aware of the dangers magical systems could pose, and are always looking for ways to keep yourself and your crew safe. How does this manifest? Is it innate, or a honed skill?*

### Bailing Wire and Twine
You get an extra **[[5. Downtime/4. Downtime Activities\|downtime activity]]** to **[[5. Downtime/5. Downtime Activities/Repair\|repair]]**, and this activity costs you **0 [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Coin]]**.

*The repair downtime activity usually takes **1 Gold** to perform. With this ability, you can take the activity at no Gold cost. The free repairs can’t be “saved up.” You get one per downtime.*

### Hidden Features
Spend your **[[1. How to Play/5. Armor & Special Armor/Special Armor]]** to initiate a flashback where you applied a temporary or experimental modification to your own or a crewmate's equipment.

*This cost replaces the normal stress cost for a flashback. This could be used to add a new property to a piece of equipment useful for this job, or it could simply be increasing it's quality (and thus effectiveness) for the remainder of the job.*

### Black Mage
When you destroy something with magic, recover **1 [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress\|stress]]**. When you **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** or **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Hack]]** a magical effect, you can spend your **[[1. How to Play/5. Armor & Special Armor/Special Armor]]** to add one of the following effects:

- It is especially destructive
- It is entirely inconspicuous
- Your crew is not affected

*A black mage tends to focus on removing obstacles instead of analyzing them. Rig can be used to construct magical effects, while Hack can be used to subvert them.*

### Junkyard Hunter
When **[[5. Downtime/5. Downtime Activities/Acquire\|Acquiring]]** an item or piece of equipment, increase its **[[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Loadout#Quality\|Quality]]** by 1 or apply a special feature to it for free

*Your junkyard contacts can get what you need refurbished or on special offer, and you can take care of the rest.*

### Mechanic's Heart
When you speak from your heart, your words can reach even the most hardened criminal, and you gain **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect#Potency\|potency]]**.

*This ability works in all situations without restriction. As long as you speak genuinely and sincerely, your words will be heard.*

### Overcharge
When you spend a **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/5. Gambits\|gambit]]** on a **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** roll to repair or upgrade, treat the system you worked on as **1 [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Loadout#Quality\|Quality]]** higher for the remainder of the job.

*You may make a **Rig** roll during a job to simply to get more out of a system, but such temporary boosts are only situational and need to be backed out the next time you hit drydock. You can overclock systems that aren’t ships, enhancing the system’s quality.*

### Eccentric
You have as many hobbies as you have ideas. When you make a **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll\|threat roll]]** with 1 or fewer dice, the cost to **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/2. Push Yourself]]** is **1 [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress]]** (instead of 2).

*This ability helps shore up your weaker actions, allowing you to more reliably get a good effect or reduce the consequences you suffer.*

# Mechanic Items
Mechanics have tools, ship parts, and their latest inventions around or on them. Although they can dress like anyone else, many mechanics prefer looks that are tough to tear, have plenty of places to stash a tool or two, and are easy to crawl through a tight space in.

### Genius Pet
Incapable of speaking, but can understand language and assist with basic tasks. Likes you. Really cute. Anticipates your actions. **\[0 Load\]**

*Is it something familiar (like a dog or a cat), exotic (like an owl or rat), or completely alien (like an imp or faerie)? What is its name? Who did you get it from (legally or illegally)?*

### [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Loadout#Quality\|Masterwork]] Airship Repair Tools
Power-assisted wrenches, a sonic drill, testing probes, thaumaturgical calibrators, an arc welder. **\[2 Load\]**

*Is this a set of items, or did you pick them up piecemeal?*

### [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Loadout#Quality\|Masterwork]] Hexcracking Tools
Scanners, identity spells, modified magitech manipulators, illegally salvaged tech. Hexcracking is as much about hardware as it is about the magic. **\[1 Load\]**

*Who maintains your gear? Do you write your own spells or does someone supply you with the latest? Any decorations in your kit?*

### Remote Scry Drone
A small magitech scrying drone that can be remotely viewed through as though it were a familiar. May be able to carry something light. **\[0 Load\]**

*Did you make this or buy it? Does it fly, slither, or crawl? What nickname did you give it? Do you have several drones on the ship, or just a bunch of chassis and repair bits you use to repair one drone?*

### Spare Parts
Usually for [[8. Glossary/Airship\|airship]] repairs and magitech. Often forgotten in a pocket or tool belt. Although it's usually nothing overly expensive, you end up carrying parts you're working on and pieces for fixing airships. If you ever need a wire, solder, or a piece of pipe, you have it on hand. Anything rare or more complex might take a lifestyle roll to have on you. **\[1 Load\]**

### Weavesight Goggles
Eyewear with settings that allow you to see across multiple spectrums, including ultra-low light, thermal, and magic-sensing. **\[1 Load\]**

*What do they look like?*
