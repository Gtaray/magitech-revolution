When you **Helm**, you pilot a vehicle or use vehicle weapons. You might cruise a tight canyon in your [[8. Glossary/Airship\|airship]]. You might be fighting in an [[8. Glossary/Exosuit\|exosuit]]. You might lose ballista bolts from your [[8. Glossary/Airship\|airship's]] broadside. You might overload the engines on your [[8. Glossary/Airship\|airship]] to get out of a firing arc (though **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** may be better)
## GM Questions

- How do you control your vehicle? How do you know how it’ll respond?
- Where do you want to end up?
- How often have you used one of these?

When you **Helm** a ship or vehicle, your guidance determines where the craft goes. In open space, there’s not a lot of meaningful choice. But in dense traffic around a city, cutting through canyons while escaping pursuers, or riding storm winds in a race across the desert, the ability to **Helm** is critical.

You can **Helm** any sort of vehicle (like boats!), including animals trained for riding. Rolling **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Sway]]** may work better for those though.

You can also use **Helm** to fire vehicle weapons, such as those commonly found on [[8. Glossary/Airship\|airships]]. Most of these weapons are at least somewhat magical, and require familiarity with the weapon more than quick reflexes or keen eyes. For "dumb" weapons that are simply mounted on a vehicle, you might consider **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Scrap]]** instead.

[[8. Glossary/Airship\|Airships]] are designed to stay afloat in most conditions while out in the open; in general, ordinary piloting does not require a roll. But [[8. Glossary/Airship\|airships]] operate best when between 500 and 2000 feet of [[8. Glossary/Airship#Altitude\|altitude]]; going above or below that for any length of time will likely require a **Helm** roll.

There is little risk involved when traveling on established trade lanes, however those trade lanes are well-guarded. Going off the beaten path could prove useful, but you'll have to manually read and predict weather patterns, respond to unexpected hazards (like a wyvern that's out hunting), and worst case, find a patch of terrain large and flat enough for you to ground in to recharge your engines and make repairs. Lots of room for **Helm** checks.
# Examples
## Controlled

> *I want to swing our ship around so we can bring our broadside weapons to bear against their rear arc.*

**4/5 Minor Complication:** You manage to swing around to their rear arc and begin to line up your shots, but you see a squad of gliders depart from their ship, and they're headed your way!

**4/5 Reduced [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect\|Effect]]:** They're more nimble than you expected. You get your guns in arc, but so are theirs!

## Risky

> *When the enemy ship moves in close to hit us with their flamethrowers, I’m gonna do a hard burn try to ram them with our mining drill.*

**4/5 [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Harm]] and Complication:** You hit hard burn and get in close. Fire sprays across the deck of your ship, igniting the deck and several components, before you impact their ship and begin to drill through their hull. The fire mixed with the ram is too much. Your ship takes two damage to **Hull**, and an engine fire breaks out. Want to resist either of these?

**4/5 Reduced [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect\|Effect]]:** You move into ram, but you have to pull away at the last moment to avoid a direct spray of fire. Your drill grazes the side of their hull, doing a bit of damage, but not enough to stop them.

## Desperate

> *I grab a glider and leap off the ship. I want to zip over to the Squadron gunship, fly through one of their hatches and land in their hold. From there I can disable their primary weapon.*

**4/5 Serious Consequences:** You glide right through their hatch, but are just a moment too late to retract your glider, and it smashes into the frame of the hatch. You have no way to get back to your ship, and you hear boots on the deck above. What do you do?
