When you **Hack**, you interact with magitech in ways that their creators don't want you to, and often in ways that are destructive. You might disable a construct guarding a vault. You might bypass the wards preventing you from getting through a door. You might modify a [[8. Glossary/Firearms\|firearm's]] magic so that it explodes when used (though **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]]** might be better).

## GM Questions

- How do you gain access to the thing you are going to Hack?
- What do you want the magic or magitech to do now?
- What tools and spells are you using?

When you **Hack** something, you override the basic operations of a persistent magical enchantment. This means you usually need to physically be able to access the thing that is enchanted. It also means the thing you're hacking needs to be a piece of magic or magitech. You can't **Hack** a mundane lock, or a a sword. With this limitation you'd think Hack would be the least preferred action for solving problems, but most magic systems are all over the place in [[7. Duradin/1. Dwarven Republic of Duradin\|Duradin]], and as long as you can touch it, you can **Hack** it.

The higher the Tech rating of a city the more likely things that can be **Hacked** are common or available. A safe in a Tech 1 city might be a heavy steel box with a mechanical lock. While a safe in a Tech 3 city probably has magical wards and an advanced magitech lock. Most things become more automated and enchanted and thus prone to **Hacking**.

To **Hack**, you need a suite of tools: mostly the same things you would need to build magitech device, though customized to your particular style. Enchantments are regularly refreshed and updated, so you can assume that an exploit that works in one place does not work everywhere. However, you can buy spells designed to break magitech - using an **[[5. Downtime/5. Downtime Activities/Acquire]]** [[5. Downtime/4. Downtime Activities\|downtime activity]] to pick up a custom hacking spell will grant potency.

Consequences from **Hacking** tend to be complications rather than physical harm. Magitech often raises alarms. Attempts could take extra time. Unexpected parts of the system might be affected. Additional **Heat** is frequent, as you leave behind unique traces of your unique style. [[8. Glossary/Relic Engine\|Relic engines]] are not easily **Hacked**. They require a combination of [[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Rig]] and [[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Attune]] to affect.

# Examples

## Controlled

> _I want to **Hack** into the Magnar's magically warded journal and see if I can get to the juicy details inside._

**4/5 Reduced [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect\|Effect]]:** You unlock the clasp holding the book shut, but there's an enchantment inside that scrambles the text unless the [[8. Glossary/Magnar]] opens the book. You'll need him to open the book for you somehow, or **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Study]]** it to decrypt the text.

**4/5 Risky [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/3. Position\|Position]]:** You unlock the journal and start reading through. How much does the [[8. Glossary/Magnar]] write in his journal? As it's wrapping up you hear a knock at the door. "Uh, sir?" asks a voice. "We got an alert from in there and wanted to check and see if everything is okay." What do you do?

**1-3 Withdraw or press on, seizing a risky opportunity:** The journal is quite well protected, and to crack it you'll have to use some indelicate but effective methods. You can break the wards and get the journal open, but it will raise alarms throughout the estate. Want to chance it?

## Risky

> _I have access to the brig's cell controls and set up my Hacking equipment. "I'll have you out in a second!" I say._

**4/5 Complication:** The lock is simple but its security is robust. You break through the enchantments, but as the door opens, the lights turn red and klaxons sound. You hear the sound of shouting and booted feet from down the hall. What do you do?

**4/5 Desperate [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/3. Position\|Position]]:** You spring the rest of the crew, but the comms squawks to life. "Hey who's there? I don't show a prisoner transfer record for today. Name and rank." Do you talk to him, or let the chips fall where they may?

## Desperate

> _We've gotta shut down that cannon firing on us. Can I use some cover to get close enough to Hack it at range?_

**4/5 Severe [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Harm]]:** No time for finesse, you get as close as you can and start running through your suite of spells, and are eventually able to shut it down, giving you the chance to get back to ship. You're almost there when the operators are able to get the cannon working again. A shell only clips you, but it's a freaking cannon. You make it to the ship, but bleeding heavily. Take level 3 harm "Cannon fire." Want to resist?
