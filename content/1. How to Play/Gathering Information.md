When a player wants to know something about the world around their character, tell them what’s obvious to them, including what they **know**, what they **intuit**, and what they **suspect** might be true, based on the appropriate aspect of their character, like their playbook, background, heritage, special items, or abilities.

> *“You’re a seasoned Scoundrel, so you can spot every point of infiltration here. But the upper balcony looks the most promising. There’s a walkway along an elevated street where you could drop down and not be seen. Of course, the Count is supposed to have good security, so there’s probably really good locks up there, at least.”*

If there’s more to discover, ask them if they want to know more, and what their character does to find out.

Based on their approach and the situation at hand, choose one:
- Offer a **[[1. How to Play/Devil's Bargain]]**: either a **cost** or a **threat**
- Give them a **lead**
- Ask them to start a **[[5. Downtime/Downtime Activities/Work#Long-Term Project\|long-term project]]** to investigate the information

## Devil's Bargain
**Cost:** Offer a **[[1. How to Play/Devil's Bargain]]** with a cost when they can simply get the information for a price. **[[2. The Characters/Rules/Coin]]**, **[[4. The Job/Heat]]**, **[[1. How to Play/Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress]]**, or ticking a **[[1. How to Play/The Basics/Progress Clocks\|clock]]** are good options, depending on the nature of the scoundrel’s action.

> *“Helene at the Silver Stag will tell you whatever you want to know about her guests, for the right price. 2 coin should do it.”*
> 
> *“You can beat the tar out of him and he tells you everything, but there are witnesses all around. You’ll take 1 Heat.”*

**Threat:** Offer a **[[1. How to Play/Devil's Bargain]]** with a **[[1. How to Play/Threat Roll/Threat Roll\|threat]]** when there’s risk or danger involved, describing the potential **[[1. How to Play/Harm, Stress & Trauma/Consequences\|consequence]]** they face to get the information.

> “The bank is patrolled by elite guards at all hours. During your visit, you can Survey the place to figure out its security systems, but **the guards might become suspicious** unless you’re subtle about it—the threat is **two Alert ticks** before the score even starts.”

If the information is especially protected or concealed, the **[[1. How to Play/Threat Roll/Threat Roll#The Threat of Failure\|threat of failure]]** may come into play, but use this sparingly. It’s usually better for players to make discoveries and use the information to drive the game forward instead of being stalled or confused.

> *“You can [[2. The Characters/Actions/Attune]] to perceive echoes of recent events, but the killing happened here years ago. Can [[2. The Characters/Actions/Attune]] be used to reach back that far? I think that’s a stretch, but it sounds cool, so let’s find out with a roll. There are two threats: **you might not perceive anything clearly from so long ago (failure)**, and the strain of trying to look back that far **overwhelms your mind** (harm 2).”*

## Leads
Give the player a **lead** when they’re not in a position to gather the information yet, but there’s a person, place, or thing in the world that can give them the opportunity.

> *“You don’t know anything about the permits and licenses that you’d need to acquire (or fake) to get started on this scam, but your friend Petra works in the city records archives. She probably knows a lot about that stuff.”*

## Long-Term Project
If the information is complex or time-consuming to gather, ask them to start a [[5. Downtime/Downtime Activities/Work#Long-Term Project\|long-term project]] to study the subject during downtime. There’s no cost or threat — it’s just a matter of time and access to the appropriate research materials or contacts.

*“You start going through the captain’s log books, and quickly realize it’s written in code. You can [[2. The Characters/Actions/Study]] it and try to crack the cypher, but you’ll need to work on it as a long-term project.”*

If you don’t have the materials or contacts (or want better ones), you might acquire an **asset**, pay a **cost**, or face a **threat** to get them.

*“If you can acquire the official port logs at the skydock, you could compare what’s in there to what she’s written... that’ll give you a bonus tick to the project clock every time you work on it. Same goes if you can get one of her crew members to recount the trip in detail — but there’s a threat there if the crew talks to the captain about it.”*
