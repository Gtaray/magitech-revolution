**Pushing Yourself** is a common game mechanic that allows your character to go beyond the normal limitations of their abilities.

There are three ways to **push yourself**:

- Reduce or avoid a consequence of a [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll#Threat Roll\|threat roll]]
- Increase the **effect** of an action you take
- Activate **special abilities**

When you **push yourself**, make an **[[1. How to Play/2. Actions & Attributes/Actions#Attribute Rolls\|attribute]]** roll that corresponds to the type of action you're taking or consequence you’re suffering, and gain stress based on the result according to the table below.

| Roll    | Outcome                  |
| ------- | ------------------------ |
| **6 6** | You take **zero Stress** |
| **6**   | You take **1 Stress**    |
| **4/5** | You take **2 Stress**    |
| **1-3** | You take **3 Stress**    |
# Resistance Roll
When you suffer a consequence, you can choose to **push yourself** to avoid or reduce it (depending on the outcome of the [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll#Threat Roll\|Threat Roll]]).  This is often referred to as **resisting** a consequence or effect, or as a **resistance roll**.

- Roll **[[1. How to Play/2. Actions & Attributes/Attributes#Insight\|Insight]]** against threats involving disorientation, confusion, doubt, or uncertainty.
- Roll **[[1. How to Play/2. Actions & Attributes/Attributes#Prowess\|Prowess]]** against threats resulting from pushing physical limits (strength, speed, endurance), exhaustion, or deprivations.
- Roll **[[1. How to Play/2. Actions & Attributes/Attributes#Resolve\|Resolve]]** against threats involving temptation, lack of self-control, willpower, hesitation, or loss of nerve.

If you face  multiple consequences, you can push yourself against each one separately.
# Increase Effect of an Action

After taking an action or making a [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll#Threat Roll\|threat roll]], you can choose to **push yourself** to increase the **effect** of that action. Because this is done after the roll, you will always know whether the increased effect is something you want.

When you do this, instead of making an **attribute** roll, you can instead choose to take **2 Stress**
# Activate a Special Ability
Some playbook abilities require that you **push yourself** to activate them. In this case you would roll and gain the stress prior before using the ability.

When you do this, instead of making an **attribute** roll, you can instead choose to take **2 Stress**
