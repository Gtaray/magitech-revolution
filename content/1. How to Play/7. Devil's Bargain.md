When a scoundrel wants something, and they can’t simply achieve it, offer them a **Devil’s Bargain** to get it by telling them either:

- The **cost** to get what they want
- A **threat** they need to face
## Costs
- Mark **[[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress\|stress]]**
- Spend **[[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Coin\|coin]]**
- Lose an item
- Tick a **[[1. How to Play/1. The Basics/3. Progress Clocks\|clock]]**
- Suffer **[[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Harm\|harm]]**
- Take **[[4. The Job/6. Heat\|Heat]]**
- Lose **[[6. Factions/3. Faction Status\|faction status]]**

Offer a cost to skip past troubles without spending a lot of time and attention at the table. They pay the cost, and you move on.

> *“Yeah, you can pry the window open and sneak in, simple enough, but there’s a bit of noise... I’ll **tick the Alert clock**.”*
> 
> *“You spend all night studying the files and it all makes sense. Take **level 1 harm: ‘Exhausted’** and you learn who the secret agent is.”*

A cost can be minor (1 Stress, one clock tick) or major (losing faction status) depending on the nature of the scoundrel’s goal. If a player doesn’t agree to the cost, they can instead ask the GM if they can face a threat or undertake some other course of action to get what they want. 

When you offer a Stress cost, you can simply name the number (usually just 1 or 2) or ask the scoundrel to **push themselves** to do it.

## Threats
Offer a threat to increase engagement and attention — leading to a threat roll, pushing, teamwork, etc. If a player doesn’t want to face the threat, ask them to revise their action to try things a different way.

> *“You can steal the documents from Master Slane’s office, but he has good security — **you might get caught in the act**.”*
> 
>*“Maybe I can convince one of his men to snatch them for me?”*
> 
> *“Yeah, you could [[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Sway]] or [[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Command]] one of them, but he might **panic and tell Slane** that you’re behind the theft when it’s noticed. Or just pay him off with **2 [[2. The Characters/4. Rules/Coin\|coin]]** and that’ll settle it.”*
### During a Threat Roll
If a PC is already facing a threat and making a [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll\|roll]], and they want a better chance of success, they can ask for a Devil’s Bargain to add an [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll#Additional Threats\|additional threat]], giving them **+1d** to roll.

Of course, after the roll, the player has to give the Devil their due and assign one of their dice results to the additional threat to see if they suffer that consequence.

A player can also ask for greater [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect\|effect]] or an additional achievement by making a Devil’s Bargain.

> *“I was hoping that my sleep detonator could take out everyone in the room at once... can we make a Devil’s Bargain?”*
> 
> *“Sure! Either there’s a **cost**: mark two more uses of your bandolier, or I can add a **threat**: you overload the detonator and might get hit with the spell, too.”*

## The Devil is Watching
This form of the Devil’s Bargain can be used at any time in play, not just when a player makes a roll. It’s the GM’s job to listen for times when the crew’s action and goals warrant a cost or lead them into the path of a threat.

Players can also speak for the Devil — suggesting bargains for their own scoundrel or other players’ characters, especially in dangerous situations. It can be fun for the players to suggest threats for each other, effectively giving each other bonus dice by raising the stakes.

> *“You’re using your gun next to tanks of bottled lightning, right? Seems dangerous... **a tank might get ruptured and explode!**”*

On the flip side, a player might propose a cost as a Devil’s Bargain to quickly resolve something they’re less interested in playing out in detail.

> *“We don’t have a ton of time left in the session tonight... can we just say that the Cobalt Syndicate hassles us for a bit — we each take 1 Stress — then leave us alone? I’d like to keep focused on the main part of the score and try to wrap it up tonight if possible.”*
