When you’re in position to help a teammate, tell the GM what benefit you’re hoping for and say what you do to help. The GM has final say on which benefit you get, then they tell you what’s required to do it. This is an extended application of the **[[1. How to Play/7. Devil's Bargain\|Devil's Bargain]]**.

### Benefits
- Improved **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect\|effect]]** or **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/3. Position\|position]]**
- **Cover** for them: they don't have to face a **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll\|threat]]**
- **Protect** them: reduce the severity of a **[[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Consequences\|consequence]]**
- **+1d** to a roll
- More details or questions when they **[[1. How to Play/8. Gathering Information\|gather info]]**
- **1 (or more) ticks** on a **[[1. How to Play/1. The Basics/3. Progress Clocks\|clock]]**

### Requirements
- **None** (you simply do it)
- Face a **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll\|threat]]**
- Suffer **[[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Harm\|harm]]**
- Take **1 [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress]]**
- **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/2. Push Yourself\|Push Yourself]]**
- **1 (or more) ticks** on a **[[1. How to Play/1. The Basics/3. Progress Clocks\|clock]]**

## Group Actions & Scale
When the group faces the same threat (like everyone sneaking past a guard patrol), each player rolls the same action (like **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Skulk]]** in this case) to see how the consequence manifests for their character.

If the situation has multiple different threats, the team can split up and tackle them individually to address the problems in a variety of ways, each using whichever action is appropriate to what they’re doing.

> “Okay, while you Skulk past the sentries, I’ll Helm the ship into position (quietly!) so you can drop down with the loot. Maybe Ruby can Study the airship patrols and make sure we’re clear to escape?”

When a team combines their efforts, they’ll usually have more **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect\|effect]]** or a better **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/3. Position\|position]]** because of an increase in **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/4. Effect#Scale\|scale]]**. If all four crew members **[[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Scrap]]** as a team, for instance, they’re equal in scale to a guard squad, so no one faces worse [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Harm\|harm]] (and a [[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/3. Position#Desperate\|desperate]] position) as they would if fighting alone.

## Leading a Team
When you lead a **group action**, you coordinate multiple members of the team to tackle a problem together. Describe how your character leads the team in a coordinated effort. Do you bark orders, give subtle hand signals, or provide charismatic inspiration?

Each PC who’s involved makes an **[[1. How to Play/3. Threat Roll/1. Threat Roll\|threat roll]]** (using the same [[1. How to Play/2. Actions & Attributes/Actions\|action]]) and the team counts **the single best result** as the overall effort for everyone who rolled. However, the character leading the group action gains **1 [[1. How to Play/4. Harm, Stress & Trauma/Stress\|stress]]** for each PC that rolled **1-3** as their best result.

> This is how you do the “We all sneak into the building” scene. Everyone who wants to sneak in rolls their [[2. The Characters/3. Actions/Skulk\|skulk]] action, and the best result counts for the whole team. The leader suffers stress for everyone who does poorly. It’s tough covering for the stragglers.

The group action result covers everyone who rolled. If you don’t roll, your character doesn’t get the effects of the action.
